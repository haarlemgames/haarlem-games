From a four-slide design document to a finished game

Crazy Fish Blocks started as a short game design document: falling blocks of fish, match three, and a bomb fish. This page shows how that idea grew, version by version, into the game you can play today.

Step 1 · The idea

The game design document

The whole game was described in four slides: a mock-up of the screen, the rules of an endless mode, the bomb fish feature and the score system. Short, clear and buildable.

What the document asked for

  • Blocks of fish fall from the top; the arrow keys move and rotate them.
  • Three fish of the same colour in a row (horizontal or vertical) make a match and score points.
  • Four fish in a square turn into a bomb fish that clears the tiles around it.
  • More kinds of fish appear over time to raise the difficulty.
  • Game over when the water is full.
  • Score: 30 points for a match of three, 60 for a square, 30 + 20 per fish for a bomb.
Step 2 · Make it playable

The first playable version

The first build did exactly what the document said, and nothing more: an 8×8 grid, falling blocks of two fish, match three, the square that becomes a bomb fish, the score and game over. Every rule was covered by automated tests before anything was made pretty. The score values from the document went into the game unchanged and are still the same today.

The fish in the first version were simple shapes drawn in code, so the rules could be tested before any art existed.

Step 3 · Iterate

How the game grew

After the first playable, the game was played, judged and improved in short rounds. The game director decided after each round what stayed, what changed and what came next.

  1. First playableThe rules from the design document, with simple fish drawn in code.
  2. Making every move feel goodChain reactions play out step by step, with praise words (Great! Awesome! Incredible!), particles, screen shake and sound. Goals were added: clear a number of one fish to wipe the whole board.
  3. New things on the boardSharks that only disappear when two of them touch, and blocks of empty water that fish can swim through to make a match.
  4. A reason to come backCompleted goals win fish for your own aquarium, with decorations, bigger tanks, a shop and achievements.
  5. The final art styleTwenty weird cartoon creatures with swim and blink animations, a brighter palette and flat, bold buttons.
  6. Ready for playersA tutorial and first-time tips, five languages (English, Dutch, German, French, Spanish), mobile touch controls and the Poki platform SDK.
  7. A name and a logoThe working title Fish Block became Crazy Fish Blocks, with its own logo in all five languages and new illustrated icons and trophies.

Three art passes, same game

The two earlier fish sets were retired from the game to keep it small and are kept in the studio's asset library. For a fair comparison these screenshots show each set in today's interface, on the same board.

Step 4 · Design vs. game

What we kept, changed and added

A design document is a starting point, not a contract. Playing the game showed what worked and what needed to change.

Step 5 · Key decisions

Two decisions that shaped the game

A second chance comes first

When the water is full, the game does not end straight away. The only thing you see is a big Revive button. The close button appears after two seconds, so the first choice a player sees is to keep playing.

We did this on purpose: a revive keeps players in the game they were enjoying, and it is the moment where an optional rewarded ad makes sense to the player. A revive clears the board and can be used once per game. The pop-up has no extra text: "Revive" says enough.

Made for players everywhere

The game is fully translated into English, Dutch, German, French and Spanish. It picks the player's browser language automatically, and the language can be changed in the settings at any time.

Even the logo is translated: every language has its own version of the title, drawn in the same style. All text lives in separate language files, so adding a new language does not touch the game itself.
